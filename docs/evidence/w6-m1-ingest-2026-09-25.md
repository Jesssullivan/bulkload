## The ingest

`Target::probe` reads the destination with the estimate's probe and resolves
its object directory. `Ingest::open(target, journals, IngestPlan, store)`
starts or reopens a session; `Ingest::resume(target, journals, pack_id,
store)` reopens one from its journal alone. The sequence, each step
journaled only once it is durable:

1. **Preflight (R-N75).** `rev-list --objects --missing=print` over the
   plan's haves. Any `?` line, or a have the destination no longer holds,
   refuses `GIT_HAVES_UNPROVABLE` / `held_tip_closure_incomplete`. A plan ref
   that already names another object refuses `GIT_DESTINATION_OCCUPIED` /
   `carry_ref_occupied`. Either refusal leaves no journal, no quarantine and
   no ref.
2. **Begin.** The plan (pack id, segment count, haves, the negotiated
   shallow frontier, ref updates and the destination's git dir) is written
   as one block ending in a `planned` record, which carries the block's
   BLAKE3, and sealed. Then
   `objects/incoming-bulkload-<pack_id>-<state key>/pack` is made and sealed
   (the key names the state dir; #75 r2 N4).
3. **Segments.** `index-pack --stdin --fix-thin --keep="bulkload git-carry-v2
   <pack_id>"` runs with the quarantine as `GIT_OBJECT_DIRECTORY`, the main
   store as its alternate, and `GIT_QUARANTINE_PATH` set, so Git refuses any
   ref update. The pack's files and the quarantine are sealed, then the
   segment is journaled with its pack hash, bytes and BLAKE3 as received.
   That record is the ack; `next_segment` is the `GitResume` value. A failed
   `index-pack` refuses `segment_invalid` and sweeps the quarantine.
4. **Connectivity.** `rev-list --objects --stdin --not --all --quiet` over
   the update oids, inside the quarantine. A failure refuses
   `connectivity_missing` and abandons the session.
5. **Migration.** Each pack's `.keep`, `.pack`, `.rev` and `.idx` (in that
   order) are renamed into `objects/pack` without replacement. Then
   `objects/pack` is sealed, the quarantine removed, and `objects/` sealed.
6. **Publish.** One `update-ref --stdin -z` transaction outside the
   quarantine. An absent ref is a `create`, a ref already at its oid a
   `verify`; nothing else is allowed. The digest of every `refs/carry/*` ref
   outside the plan is taken before and after, and recorded.
7. **Keeps.** Each migrated pack's `.keep` is dropped if it still holds this
   session's message, then `objects/pack` is sealed.

Git's own writes run with `core.fsync=committed,derived-metadata` and
`core.fsyncMethod=fsync`. Bulkload also seals every file and directory it
depends on through `durable::seal_file` and `seal_dir`.

**Journal.** `<state>/git-carry-v2/ingest/<pack_id>.journal` is
append-only. Each record ends in the first 16 hex digits of its own BLAKE3
and is written in one `write`, then sealed. The file is created
`O_EXCL|O_NOFOLLOW` at 0600 in a private directory, reopened `O_NOFOLLOW`
with the private-file checks, and held under `flock` for the life of its
session.
- A final line without its newline is a torn append: it is cut off, and
  that step is redone.
- A complete line that fails its check refuses `SCHEMA_MISMATCH`.
- A journal with no sealed plan, or an abandoned one, is removed on the next
  open, which then starts fresh.
- Reopening with a different plan, or against another repository, refuses
  `JOURNAL_OWNERSHIP_CONFLICT`, as does a second concurrent session.
- A state dir inside the destination (work tree, git dir, common dir or
  objects dir, under any spelling) refuses `SNAPSHOT_ROOTS_OVERLAP`.

**Recovery.** It re-runs from the last journaled step:
- stray `tmp_*` files and unjournaled packs are swept from the quarantine;
- migration treats a file already in `objects/pack` as moved, and refuses
  `SEALED_OBJECT_MISSING` only when a journaled pack is in neither place;
- publication re-plans a `create` as a `verify` once the ref holds its oid;
- a `.keep` that is gone is skipped.

## What the tests assert

**Every PR 1 fixture, now through the product ingest.** That covers the
spike Q1 set, #55's B, P1, M, F and A3, D1, non-UTF-8 paths and 12 random
DAGs (proptest), each with a planted `refs/carry/v1/existing/<name>` ref.
On every one:
- each segment is received and acked with its exact bytes and BLAKE3;
- the plan refs are published at their wants;
- every `refs/carry/*` ref that existed before is byte-identical after, and
  the receipt's before and after digests agree;
- no quarantine, `tmp_*` or bulkload `.keep` remains;
- `fsck --strict` passes.

**Refusals and recovery** (asserted):

**Resume in process.** On the 3-segment fixture, the destination journals
segments 0..k, and segment k is cut off mid-stream (`segment_invalid`, then
swept). After both sides restart:
- the destination resumes from its journal and asks for segment k;
- the sender reloads its plan by `pack_id` and packs only segments k.. ;
- the ingest publishes exactly `upload-pack`'s objects, and `fsck` is clean.

**Git's own maintenance.** A quarantine with files dated 2020 survived
`git gc --prune=now` and `git prune --expire=now` in the destination
(observed once on git 2.54.0): Git's cleanup does not descend into
`objects/incoming-*`. After migration each pack is under a `.keep` until
publication, so a `gc` in that window does not repack or prune it.

## Known limits

- **`_exit` models a process crash only.** It cannot detect a missing
  fsync (see `fault.rs`). The seals are placed by reading the code; the W7
  power-loss checker (R-N88) is where they get proven.
- **Receipt digest.** Since #75 r3 M1 the digest covers only existing refs
  that could alias a plan ref. Both digests are taken under a
  per-repository fence, held by `finish` and `abandon`. Since #75 r4 N3 the
  fence is an exclusive `flock` on `<common>/bulkload-ingest.lock`, a
  dedicated lock file opened read-write. The wait is bounded at about two
  seconds; past that the call refuses `JOURNAL_OWNERSHIP_CONFLICT` /
  `repository_fenced` and the caller retries. The retry and backoff
  contract is #89. Unequal digests refuse `carry_ref_moved`; a resume
  takes `before` afresh.
- **Local filesystems only (OI-1001-Q17, #75 r5).** The journal, quarantine
  and fence locks are `flock` locks. On NFS, `flock` is emulated with
  `fcntl` locks, which need a writable descriptor, so the quarantine
  directory's lock would likely fail with `EBADF`; other network
  filesystems give no better guarantee. NFS support is dropped rather than
  built. `Target::probe` classifies the destination's `objects/` and common
  dir and refuses a network filesystem with
  `GIT_DESTINATION_FILESYSTEM_UNSUPPORTED`, naming the kind, before
  anything is written:
  - Linux, by `statfs.f_type`: NFS, SMB, CIFS, SMB2, AFS, 9P, Ceph, Coda;
  - Darwin, by `statfs.f_fstypename`: `nfs`, `smbfs`, `webdav`, `afpfs`,
    `ftp`.

  The classifier is unit-tested. No lab host (sting, neo, honey, bumble)
  has an NFS mount, so a real refusal has not been exercised.
  A session past migration can be abandoned: that drops its `.keep`s and
  leaves every ref, including published plan refs, as it is.
  The whole-namespace `refs/carry/*` digest gate in the fixture tests is
  asserted externally, by the test harness.
- **Unicode ref aliases (#75 r2 N3).** An existing ref with a non-ASCII
  component is taken to alias every plan component at its depth (Darwin
  file systems fold Unicode case and normalization; no fold tables are
  carried), so a plan ref beside, under or over one refuses.
- **Orphan quarantines (#75 r2 N4, r3 M2/M3).** A fresh open discards any
  quarantine with its own name: the plan's pack id plus this state dir's
  key. That includes one left by a state dir recreated at the same path.
  A quarantine under another state dir's key is never touched. The key
  comes from the state dir's path, so a rename loses resume (#82).
- **Keeps after a refused publish.** When publish refuses after migration
  (a plan ref changed underneath), the packs stay in `objects/pack` with
  this session's `.keep` until the session is finished or abandoned.
  Since #75 r3 M1, abandoning past migration drops the `.keep`s and moves
  no ref.
- **Journal and quarantine ownership (#75 r4 N1).** A stale journal is
  truncated in place, never unlinked and recreated. After `flock`, the
  locked descriptor must still be the file at the journal's name, or the
  open retries. A live session also holds an exclusive `flock` on its
  quarantine directory. A fresh open discards a quarantine only after
  taking that lock, so a session whose journal name was replaced under it
  keeps its packs. Every losing open refuses
  `JOURNAL_OWNERSHIP_CONFLICT`, and a losing fresh open removes its own
  empty journal. Since #75 r5, `receive` checks the journal is still at
  its name before indexing and again after journaling the segment.
  Otherwise it refuses `JOURNAL_OWNERSHIP_CONFLICT` / `journal_replaced`
  instead of acking a segment a resume could not read.
