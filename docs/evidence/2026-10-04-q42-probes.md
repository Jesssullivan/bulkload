# Q42 lane L3: large-ref hazard probes, sting, 2026-10-04

**Label: informational, sting, ungated.** These are not gate samples. R-N81
power and load rules do not gate them, but every record carries its load1.

Q42 lane L3 (OI-1003-Q42 to Q45) gathers the sting-side "evidence that
would change it" for the carry_v2 deletion. carry_v2 stays frozen and was
not run (OI-1003-Q15). The neo half is deferred; see
[Pending](#pending-the-neo-read-only-estimate-pass-deferred). The
decision packet these figures answer is
`docs/plans/2026-10-04-git-engine-decision.md` on PR #173: its "Ref count
changes the picture" item, and the large-ref hazard corpus it names as one
way to settle Q45's allowance.

## Findings

1. **At 119,761 carry-shaped refs, v1 refuses the repository on every
   pass.**
   - The capture header is 20,745,391 B, 3,968,175 B over the 16 MiB cap.
   - `git-export` refuses it `GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED`, and so does
     `estate-capture`, which then exits `CONTRACT_SELF_INCONSISTENT`. Both
     refusals are typed: no panic and no bare IO.
   - A canonical carry ref costs 138 B plus its suffix per header line, so
     no carry inventory of 119,761 refs fits. The blahaj-like shape reaches
     the cap at about 96,855 refs.
2. **Grouped items have a cap window.** At 96,849 refs, a plan base fits
   (22,259 B under), but its grouped items' rewritten headers do not
   (33,880 B over). The items are captured and recorded, then refused by
   the next pass and by `estate-apply`.
3. **The edge-aggressive walk is cheap.**
   - At 119,761 refs and 1,032 excluded tips it costs 0.13 to 0.25 s of
     CPU. It lists 4 objects and packs 3,342 B, against the control's
     479,876 B.
   - A changed pass's CPU goes elsewhere: 54.0 s per chained capture at
     119,761 refs (about 0.44 ms per ref), and 1,432 s for a grouped pass at
     96,849 refs.
4. **The inputs to M (SHA-1):**
   - c ≈ 1.4 KB;
   - a = 42 B + the exported refname, per ref;
   - p = 54 B per source-held tip;
   - b ≈ 24.6 B per entry of a changed tree;
   - s ≈ 6 to 7 B packed (47 B raw) per seat: a per-seat term that Q45's
     form lacks.

   At 119,761 compact refs, a·refs is 99.05 % of the 8.85 MB bundle for a
   one-line change.
5. **The neo estimate pass is pending** (see the last section).

## Corpora

Every corpus is synthetic and deterministic (seed 42), built by
`q42_probes.py corpus` with `git fast-import`, `commit-tree` and one
`update-ref --stdin` transaction, then `pack-refs --all`.

- **Content:** each corpus has 64 directories × 64 files (4,096 tracked
  files and 4,160 seats with the directories).
- **Commits:** a linear native history of 561 commits, each changing one
  file.
- **Snapshot namespaces:** 157, as in #48's blahaj inventory. Each holds a
  copy of the native refs as they stood at that snapshot, plus `head`,
  `staged`, `worktree` and `filesystem-v1`. The last three are parentless
  commits, distinct for each namespace.

## 1. Header bytes against the 16 MiB cap

The agent reads bundle headers in four places, each with the same bounds:
lines of at most 1 MiB, and a refusal once more than 16 MiB (16,777,216 B)
has been consumed. The readers are:

- `git_carry/shared.rs` `prerequisites` (L131 at `8dc26c1`);
- `pack_object_count` (L205);
- `write_with_prerequisites` (L433);
- `git_carry/chain.rs` `advertised`.

| Corpus, verb | Header the agent wrote (B) | Ref lines | Prerequisite lines | Against the cap (B) | Reader verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `carry-119761`, `git-export` (bundle kept for diagnosis) | 20,745,391 | 119,768 | 0 | +3,968,175 over | `GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED` (header over 16 MiB) |
| `carry-119761`, `estate-capture` (private capture kept) | 20,745,391 | 119,768 | 0 | +3,968,175 over | `GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED` |
| `compact-119761`, `estate-capture` pass 1 (self-contained) | 8,761,559 | 119,768 | 0 | 8,015,657 under | ok |
| `compact-119761`, `estate-capture` pass 2 (chained) | 8,817,287 | 119,768 | 1,032 | 7,959,929 under | ok |
| `small-compact`, pass 2 (chained) | 22,352 | 263 | 64 | 16,754,864 under | ok |
| `carry-window`, plan base (shared) | 16,754,957 | 96,850 | 0 | 22,259 under | ok |
| `carry-window`, grouped item (main checkout), recorded | 16,811,096 | 96,856 | 1,032 | +33,880 over | `GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED` (header over 16 MiB) |
| `carry-window`, grouped item (linked worktree), recorded | 16,811,096 | 96,856 | 1,032 | +33,880 over | `GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED` |

The model and the agent agree. The modelled ref term plus 17 B of signature
and blank line, plus the 7 capture metadata ref lines (487 B), gives each
header the agent wrote, to the byte.

**At 119,761 refs, a carry-shaped inventory cannot fit under the cap,
whatever its native names are.** A canonical carry ref's line is
40 + 1 + 27 (`refs/carry-export/union/v1/`) + 3 (`neo`) + 1 + 64 + 1
+ suffix + 1 = **138 B + its suffix**. At 119,761 refs the ref term alone
fits only if the mean suffix is at most 2.09 B (16,777,216 ÷ 119,761 =
140.09). The shortest real suffix is `head` (4 B), so every such inventory
is over the cap. With blahaj-like suffixes (`refs/heads/...`,
`refs/remotes/origin/...`, metadata names; mean 35.7 B, at least 4 B) the line is 173 B,
and the cap is reached at **about 96,855 refs**.

The packet's estimate, "a header line of about 85 B ... about 10 MB per
changed capture per item", is too low for a carry inventory. The union
prefix, the source slug and the 64-hex snapshot digest add 78 B to every
line, over a plain ref with the same name. At
119,761 refs, v1 does not pay 10 MB per capture. It **refuses the
repository on every pass**: see section 3. A SHA-256 repository adds 24 B
per line, so the cap comes about 12 % sooner (about 85,068 refs).

For a plain inventory (`refs/carry-export/<name>`, 60 B + the name), the cap
sits at 167,772 refs for a 40 B mean name, and at 229,339 refs for the
compact corpus's 13 B names.

**Which reader trips where.** Every capture passes through
`pack_object_count` (L205) on the bundle it has just written, before any
record is kept. So a self-contained capture, a chained capture and a plan
base are all refused there, as soon as their own header is over the cap.
The other three readers are reached as follows:

- **`chain::advertised`** reads only a prior that this agent already
  accepted at L205. By construction, it cannot see an over-cap header that
  this binary wrote.
- **`write_with_prerequisites` (L433)** caps the header that *git* wrote.
  For a grouped item, git omits every ref whose tip is excluded, so that
  header is small. The header the agent then writes in its place has 54 B
  per base commit, plus every private ref, and it is **not** measured at
  write time: the stats come from git's bundle.
- **`prerequisites` (L131)** then meets that header on the next pass
  (`retained_capture`'s `requires_base`, `chainable`) and at apply
  (`import_base`). The cap-window probe in section 3 measures this
  write/read asymmetry.

## 2. The edge-aggressive walk

`write_excluding_tip_trees` runs `rev-list --objects-edge-aggressive --all
--stdin` with one `^tip` line per source-held tip of the prior. The probe
replays it in a private-like bare repository:

- its alternates name the source's objects;
- it holds every source ref under the export mapping, plus `head`;
- it adds one new parentless worktree commit, which is HEAD's tree with one
  changed blob two levels down;
- the exclusions are every distinct tip, peeled to a commit the source
  holds, as `chain::source_held_tips` builds them.

The probe ran 3 reps of each mode with the agent's pack `-c` settings.
`pack-objects --stdout --delta-base-offset` then packed the listed objects.

| Corpus | Private refs | Excluded tips | Aggressive: median wall / CPU (s) | Objects listed | Pack (B) | Control `--objects-edge`: median wall / CPU (s) | Objects listed | Pack (B) |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| `carry-119761` | 119,763 | 1,032 | 0.261 / 0.247 | 4 | 3,342 | 0.246 / 0.205 | 4,162 | 479,876 |
| `compact-119761` | 119,763 | 1,032 | 0.135 / 0.131 | 4 | 3,342 | 0.127 / 0.123 | 4,162 | 479,876 |
| `carry-window` | 96,851 | 1,032 | 0.223 / 0.213 | 4 | 3,342 | 0.197 / 0.185 | 4,162 | 479,876 |
| `small-compact` | 258 | 64 | 0.012 / 0.009 | 4 | 3,339 | 0.021 / 0.012 | 4,162 | 474,571 |

**The walk is not the large-ref hazard.** At 119,761 refs and 1,032
excluded tips it costs about 0.13 to 0.25 s of CPU. Most of that is
reading 119,763 refs for `--all`; the `^tip` exclusions cost little. The
aggressive marking costs within noise of the non-aggressive control here,
and it lists 4 objects instead of the whole tracked tree. At 4,096 tracked
files, that is a pack 144× smaller (3,342 B against 479,876 B).

The aggressive cost grows with the distinct trees under the excluded tips
(each is parsed once). At 64 × 64 that is under 2,000 trees. A much larger
tracked tree is the case these corpora do not reach (Limits).

The agent's own changed pass is where the CPU goes:

| Corpus | Pass | Wall (s) | CPU (s) | load1 before | Pack written (B) | Retained bundle re-read (B) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `small-compact` | 1, self-contained | 3.70 | 1.01 | 60.4 | 545,795 | 0 |
| `small-compact` | 2, chained | 1.47 | 1.24 | 58.5 | 56,091 | 545,795 |
| `compact-119761` | 1, self-contained | 231.22 | 47.73 | 49.2 | 9,565,764 | 0 |
| `compact-119761` | 2, chained | 57.20 | 54.04 | 24.4 | 8,845,052 | 9,565,764 |
| `carry-119761` | `git-export`, refused | 31.55 | 26.55 | 37.6 | 0 (counter; git wrote 21,621,186) | 0 |
| `carry-119761` | `estate-capture` 1, refused | 34.43 | 30.17 | 37.6 | 0 (counter) | 0 |
| `carry-window` | 1, plan base + 2 grouped items | 1,671.47 | 1,431.73 | 35.7 | 18,619,065 (git's bundles) | 0 |

A chained capture at 119,761 refs costs **54.0 s of CPU**, against 1.24 s
at 256 refs: about **0.44 ms of CPU per ref per changed capture**. The
self-contained first pass costs about 0.39 ms per ref. The walk is under
0.5 % of that.

The per-ref cost sits in the rest of the pass:

- one ref-creating transaction per private repository;
- the reuse fetch of the whole retained bundle (S3 finding 5), here
  9.57 MB, of which 8.76 MB is header;
- clearing the reuse refs;
- the repeated `for-each-ref` reads.

Even a refused pass spends 26.5 to 30.2 s of CPU before it reaches the cap.

## 3. Refusals: typed, never a panic or a bare IO

| Probe | Exit | Codes | Kind | Panic |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| `carry-119761` `git-export` | 1 | `GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED` | typed | no |
| `carry-119761` `estate-capture` | 1 | item `outcome=refused reason=GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED`; the verb exits `CONTRACT_SELF_INCONSISTENT` | typed | no |
| `carry-window` `estate-capture` pass 1 (plan base + 2 grouped items) | 0 | none: both items `outcome=captured`, each with a recorded header 33,880 B over the cap | **accepted** | no |
| `carry-window` `estate-capture` pass 2, unchanged | 1 | both items `refused reason=GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED`; the verb exits `CONTRACT_SELF_INCONSISTENT` | typed | no |
| `carry-window` `estate-apply` | 1 | both items `refused reason=GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED`; the verb exits `CONTRACT_SELF_INCONSISTENT` | typed | no |

**The cap refuses as a typed value. No run panicked (exit 101 or "panicked
at"), and none refused with a bare `IO` or `FRAME_CODEC`.** Three caveats
for S4 dispositions:

- **The code names the wrong cause.** The inventory is well formed. It is
  only large. An operator reading `GIT_INVENTORY_MALFORMED` would look for
  corruption. A size-specific code, or a reason on the existing one, would
  route the disposition correctly.
- **A refused pass leaves its write behind.** It writes the whole bundle
  (21.6 MB here) before L205 reads it back and refuses. The private capture
  is kept for diagnosis (as `export_repository` documents), and
  `write_source_pack_bytes` stays 0 for it, because `PackStats::record`
  refuses before it counts. Every pass over such a repository repeats this
  cost.
- **It recurs on every pass.** Nothing changes between passes, so the item
  is refused every time until its ref count drops below about 96,855.

**The cap window: a grouped item is written over the cap, and refused
only afterwards.** At 96,849 carry-shaped refs:

- **The plan base passes.** Its header is 16,754,957 B, 22,259 B under the
  cap.
- **Each grouped item goes over.** Its bundle declares the base's 1,032
  commits as prerequisites (55,728 B) on top of the same inventory: a
  header of 16,811,096 B, 33,880 B over.
- **Pass 1 records both items.** `write_with_prerequisites` caps only
  git's own header, which is small because git omits excluded tips, and it
  takes its stats from git's bundle. So both items are captured and
  recorded, and the verb exits 0. `write_source_pack_bytes` (18,619,065 B)
  counts git's bundles, not the rewritten ones.
- **Later readers refuse both.** The unchanged next pass refuses both at
  `requires_base` (`retained_capture`), and `estate-apply` refuses both at
  `import_base`.

Every refusal is typed, but each comes after the capture was accepted as
custody, and no later verb accepts that custody. The window exists
wherever a grouped repository's base header lies within its prerequisite
and metadata bytes of the cap: here, a base header of about 16,720,000 to
16,777,216 B.

A write-side check would refuse at capture, where the disposition belongs.
The check is to measure the final header against the same cap before the
rename, as `write_excluding_tip_trees` gets through `PackStats::record`.

**The grouped pass also costs a great deal of CPU.** Pass 1 (the plan base
and 2 items) took 1,671 s wall and **1,432 s CPU** at 96,849 refs.
`prerequisite_commits` (`shared.rs` L66 to L97) spawns a `cat-file -e` and a
`rev-parse --verify` child for every base head: 96,850 heads × 2 items. A
400-pair micro-measure in the same repository took 4.78 ms of CPU per pair,
which projects to about 927 s, two-thirds of the pass. That attribution
comes from the code and the micro-measure; the pass was not profiled. One
`cat-file --batch-check` over all heads, as `chain::source_held_tips`
already does, would remove it.

## 4. The inputs to Q45's byte allowance M

`q42_probes.py summarize` splits each chained capture bundle into terms,
from its header and from `verify-pack -v` after `bundle unbundle` into a
scratch repository that reads the source's objects. The terms add up to
the bundle size exactly (`check` = 0) in both corpora.

| Term | `compact-119761`, pass 2 (B) | `small-compact`, pass 2 (B) | Unit cost | What scales it |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| c: signature and blank line | 17 | 17 | 17 B (SHA-1), 39 B (SHA-256, with `@object-format`) | per capture |
| c: pack header and trailer | 32 | 32 | 32 B | per capture |
| c: the capture's own metadata objects (staged, worktree, configuration, exclude, symbolic HEAD, filesystem-v1 commits; their small trees and blobs) | 851 | 851 | about 851 B | per capture |
| c: the 7 metadata ref lines | 487 | 487 | about 70 B each | per capture |
| **c total** | **1,387** | **1,387** | **about 1.4 KB** | per capture |
| **a·refs**: one header line per private ref | 8,761,055 | 18,392 | **42 B + exported refname**: 73.15 B compact, 173.2 B carry-shaped (138 B + suffix) | refs, every changed capture |
| **p·tips**: one prerequisite line per source-held distinct tip | 55,728 | 3,456 | **54 B** (SHA-1), 78 B (SHA-256) | distinct tips of the prior (chained) or base commits (grouped) |
| **b·trees**: changed worktree trees, stored whole (non-thin pack) | 3,152 (2 trees, 128 entries) | 3,151 | **1,576 B per tree at 64 entries; 24.6 B per entry** | changed trees × their entries |
| **s·seats**: the filesystem-v1 rows blob, rewritten whole on every changed capture | 23,627 | 29,602 | **47.0 B raw, 5.7 to 7.1 B in pack, per seat** | every seat in the checkout, not the changed ones |
| content: the changed blob | 103 | 103 | the changed bytes | changed seats |
| **Bundle** | **8,845,052** | **56,091** | | |

Pass 2's input was one appended line in one tracked file, two levels
down. Its wire cost, as a share of the bundle:

- **compact-119761: 99.05 % header refs, 0.63 % prerequisites, 0.27 % seat
  rows**, and 103 B of content;
- **small-compact: 32.8 % refs, 52.8 % seat rows**.

**Evidence that would change Q45's form, M = c + a·refs + b·trees:**

1. **A per-seat term is missing.** filesystem-v1 holds every carried seat's
   row, so every changed capture rewrites it whole: about 47 B raw and 6 to
   7 B packed per seat. At 4,160 seats, that is 24 to 30 KB: the largest
   term of a small-ref capture, and most of S3's 28 to 40 KB "capture
   metadata" residual. So M needs an s·seats term, or filesystem-v1 needs a
   delta form.
2. **A per-tip term is missing.** A chained capture declares one 54 B
   prerequisite line per source-held distinct tip of its prior; a grouped
   item, one per base commit. That is 55.7 KB at 1,032 tips. It scales with
   distinct tips, not refs: 119,761 refs over 1,032 tips here.
3. **b is per entry, not per tree.** A changed tree is packed whole (24.6 B
   per entry), so b·trees should be b·Σ(entries of changed trees).
4. **a·refs is bounded by the cap.** Past about 96,855 carry-shaped refs
   (about 167,772 plain refs at 40 B names) a·refs is not a cost but a
   refusal. M is only meaningful under the cap. Above it, the item needs a
   typed disposition (S4) or a v1 change, such as excluding the carry
   namespaces from the header or capturing them by digest.
5. **CPU scales with refs too: about 0.44 ms per ref per changed capture.**
   A byte allowance does not bound it; S2's +2.0 load1 budget is the
   relevant bound.

With those terms, the measured inputs are:

    M ≈ 1.4 KB + (42 B + refname)·refs + 54 B·tips + 24.6 B·Σentries(changed trees) + 6–7 B·seats   (SHA-1)

## What this changes in the Q15 packet's list

- **"Ref count changes the picture": yes, more sharply than the packet
  estimated.**
  - At 119,761 carry-shaped refs, v1 does not cost about 10 MB per
    changed capture. It refuses the repository, typed, on every pass.
  - Below the cap, the header is about 99 % of a one-line change's bundle,
    and the chained pass costs about 54 s of CPU.
  - v2 negotiates refs instead of listing them in every capture, so this
    is a measured case for v2 or hybrid negotiation of the ref layer.
  - A v1 header change (carry namespaces by digest) is the alternative.
- **The edge-aggressive walk is not a reason to keep v2.** It is cheap at
  119,761 refs, and it is what keeps chained v1 bundles small.
- **Two v1 defects to add to the follow-up list, both at large ref counts.**
  - A grouped item's final header is not checked against the cap at write.
    It is recorded, then refused by every later reader (section 3).
  - `prerequisite_commits` spawns two git children per base head: about
    0.93 ks of CPU projected at 96,850 heads × 2 items.
  - Follow-up 1 rewrites that same group-base path, so both fit there.
- **The ref term is unrelated to follow-ups 1 to 4.** Follow-up 1
  (group-base tip-tree exclusion) and follow-up 2 (grouped chaining) leave
  a·refs and p·tips untouched. Follow-up 4 (reuse without the whole
  retained bundle) would remove the 9.57 MB re-read here.

## Limits

- The corpora are synthetic. The ref shape follows #48's blahaj counts:
  119,761 refs over 1,032 distinct oids, in 157 namespaces of about 750
  native refs. The native names are generated, at a mean suffix of 35.7 B.
  blahaj's real suffix lengths were not measured. The cap conclusion at
  119,761 refs does not depend on them, because any suffix of 3 B or more is
  over.
- Tracked trees are 64 × 64. The aggressive walk's cost grows with the
  distinct trees under the excluded tips, and a tree of tens of thousands
  of files was not probed.
- One chained step was measured. Chain depth, re-base and grouped chaining
  are the S3 harness's (#173) and the neo pass's.
- The host was heavily loaded (load1 24 to 87). Walls are inflated, so the
  CPU column is the comparison.
- `git-carry-estimate` (the v2 projection) was not run on these corpora.
  Its ref handling is negotiation, not a header, and the neo pass below
  runs it on real repositories.

## Pending: the neo read-only estimate pass (deferred)

**State: pending.** neo is under memory pressure (swap nearly exhausted), so
nothing in this lane ran on neo. The pass runs once the operator clears neo.
It gathers the neo items from the Q15 packet's "What evidence would change
it" list (`docs/plans/2026-10-04-git-engine-decision.md` on PR #173). Every
command below is read-only on the sources:

- `git-carry-estimate` moved nothing in S2 on any pass (S3 evidence,
  finding 10);
- every plain `git` call runs through `g` (below): optional locks off, no
  automatic gc or maintenance, no fsmonitor, and no `status` index refresh
  write.

`estate-capture` is **not** run on neo in this pass. It freshens source
objects until #172 lands.

### Setup (neo)

```bash
# Private scratch on neo's scratch volume, outside every repository.
S="$TMPDIR/q42-neo-estimate"; mkdir -m 700 "$S" "$S/src" "$S/state" "$S/out"
g() { GIT_OPTIONAL_LOCKS=0 git --no-optional-locks -c gc.auto=0 \
      -c maintenance.auto=false -c core.fsmonitor=false "$@"; }

# Darwin build of the same main, own target, R-N69 prefix, background class.
git -C ~/git/bulkload archive 8dc26c19691efc8f37665b19d9b63f795103ec38 | tar -x -C "$S/src"
cd "$S/src" && TINYLAND_ALLOW_LOCAL_BUILD="q42-l3-neo (R-N69)" nice -n 19 \
  env CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=4 CARGO_TARGET_DIR="$S/target" \
  nix develop .#default --command cargo build --release --locked -p bulkload-agent
A="$S/target/release/bulkload-agent"

# The agent repositories: the sources of the current neo estate plan, one
# absolute worktree path per line (from `"$A" estate-show "$PLAN"`).
"$A" estate-show "$PLAN" > "$S/plan.txt"   # then extract source paths into:
#   "$S/repos.txt"
while read -r r; do g -C "$r" rev-parse --path-format=absolute --git-common-dir; done \
  < "$S/repos.txt" | sort -u > "$S/commons.txt"
g init --quiet --bare --template= "$S/empty.git"   # an empty destination
```

Samples are taken at t0, at t1 = t0 + 24 h and at t2 = t0 + 7 d. Each one
records the host, load1, power and UTC time.

### Metric 1: rewrite+gc fallback frequency

A chained v1 capture excludes only the retained capture's tips that the
source still holds. If a rebase or force-push moves a tip and a gc or prune
then drops the old one, the next capture falls back toward re-packing.

```bash
# t0: every tip of every common repository.
while read -r c; do
  k=$(printf %s "$c" | shasum -a 256 | cut -c1-16)
  g --git-dir="$c" for-each-ref --format='%(objectname) %(refname)' > "$S/out/tips-t0-$k"
done < "$S/commons.txt"
# Hourly (background), the gc indicator: the pack listing v1's custody path compares.
#   ls -li "$c/objects/pack" | shasum -a 256   >> "$S/out/packs-$k.log"
# t1 and t2, for each t0 line "OLD REF":
#   moved:     the ref now names another oid (or is gone)
#   rewritten: moved, and `g --git-dir="$c" merge-base --is-ancestor OLD NEW` fails
#   dropped:   `printf '%s^{commit}\n' OLD | g --git-dir="$c" cat-file --batch-check` says missing
# Fallback bytes, for a repository with a dropped tip: the excess of
#   g --git-dir="$c" rev-list --objects --all --not <held t0 tips> \
#     | cut -d' ' -f1 | g --git-dir="$c" cat-file --batch-check='%(objectsize:disk)' | awk '{s+=$1} END {print s}'
# over the same sum with every t0 tip excluded.
```

Report the following:

- rewritten tips, dropped tips and fallback events per repository per day;
- fallback bytes per event;
- pack-listing changes per repository per day.

### Metric 2: chained-item history size

Until Q15 follow-up 3, a chained item re-bases every ninth changed capture
(`CHAIN_DEPTH_LIMIT` 8), at a cost of its history ÷ 9 per changed capture.

```bash
while read -r r; do
  "$A" git-carry-estimate --state-dir "$S/state" "$r" "$S/empty.git"   # full history
  "$A" git-carry-estimate --state-dir "$S/state" "$r" "sting:<dest>"   # the v2 delta
  g -C "$r" for-each-ref | wc -l                                       # refs (a-term input)
  g -C "$r" ls-files -z | tr -cd '\0' | wc -c                          # tracked seats (s-term input)
done < "$S/repos.txt" > "$S/out/history.txt"
```

Report these per item:

- `missing_thin_pack_bytes` against the empty destination: the history a
  re-base packs;
- the re-base price, that figure ÷ 9;
- `missing_thin_pack_bytes` against sting: the v2 delta;
- refs and seats;
- the change rate: the share of samples in which HEAD, any ref or the dirty
  set moved.

### Metric 3: grouped growth

Until follow-up 2, a grouped item (several planned worktrees of one common
repository) re-packs all history since its first pass on every changed
capture, once per item. Until follow-up 1, it also re-packs every tracked
blob, because the group base has no tip-tree exclusion.

```bash
while read -r c; do
  g --git-dir="$c" worktree list --porcelain | grep -c '^worktree '      # worktrees
done < "$S/commons.txt"
# Tracked blob bytes per planned worktree (the follow-up 1 term):
#   g -C "$wt" ls-tree -r --format='%(objectname)' HEAD \
#     | g -C "$wt" cat-file --batch-check='%(objectsize:disk)' | awk '{s+=$1} END {print s}'
# History since t0 per common repository (the follow-up 2 term), at t1 and t2:
#   g --git-dir="$c" rev-list --objects --all --not $(cut -d' ' -f1 "$S/out/tips-t0-$k") \
#     | cut -d' ' -f1 | g --git-dir="$c" cat-file --batch-check='%(objectsize:disk)' | awk '{s+=$1} END {print s}'
```

Grouped growth at t1 or t2 is the sum over groups of planned items × history
since t0. It is reported next to the group base size.

### Metric 4: persistent dirty share

v2 has no snapshot layer, so a worktree that stays dirty across syncs is
state that only v1 (or a hybrid) carries.

```bash
while read -r r; do
  g -C "$r" status --porcelain=v2 -z --untracked-files=all > "$S/out/status-$(printf %s "$r" | shasum -a 256 | cut -c1-16)-tN"
done < "$S/repos.txt"
# dirty: any entry. Dirty bytes: the sizes of the listed worktree paths (stat).
```

Report the following:

- the persistent dirty share: items dirty at t0, t1 and t2, out of all items;
- the dirty byte share: dirty bytes out of tracked bytes, per sample.
